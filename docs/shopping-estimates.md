# Shopping estimates and budget comfort

Shopping ingredient estimates allocate each scaled recipe's existing total according to ingredient quantity and authored, indicative USD unit-cost weights. For example, a recipe's chicken receives a larger cost share than a few tablespoons of oil. The weights are planning assumptions, not live retailer prices. Mass and recipe-volume units can be converted within their own families; package sizes and mass-to-volume conversions are never guessed.

Allocation uses largest-remainder cent rounding so ingredient totals exactly match the selected recipe estimates, including fractional servings and merged ingredients. A recipe with any unsupported ingredient or unit retains equal shares for its complete estimate. The shopping page discloses this fallback and explains that amounts represent recipe portions; buying full packages may cost more. Saved shopping estimates remain unchanged until shopping is regenerated.

Automatic assignment only includes guests who volunteered to bring groceries. It checks each known item against that guest's saved budget comfort, including the scaled estimates of Potluck dishes they already contribute. Guests without a stated comfort limit can cover remaining costs. Unknown-price items stay unassigned for review. Assignment preserves list order and deterministic ties; it tries cost-balanced placement first and a constrained-capacity pass if some items do not fit. This is a bounded heuristic, not an optimal packing guarantee; groceries are never split solely to satisfy a budget.

The shopping page shows assigned totals against comfort limits and distinguishes unassigned items that cannot fit, missing volunteers, and unpriced items. Hosts and guests may deliberately claim or reassign items beyond comfort; preserved assignments and changed contributions can also exceed it. These cases remain editable and visibly show the amount over comfort. The limits apply to approximate food costs, not a promised retailer checkout cap. Existing saved assignments are not retroactively redistributed.
